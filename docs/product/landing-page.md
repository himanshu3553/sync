## 1. Positioning — outcomes on the homepage, the category on its own page

The category-claim homepage ("Application Intelligence Layer for your SaaS", founder brief 2026-08-13) was **superseded 2026-08-24** by a second founder brief: the claim was right at the core but **too technical for buyers** — it earned "I haven't seen this category before" from educated readers and a bounce from a buyer scanning for "will this fix my activation / ticket problem". The split that replaced it:

- **The homepage sells the USP in buyer words, mapped to the user lifecycle.** Hero (locked, founder-approved 2026-08-24, wording and color amended by the founder the same day): the whole H1 in the soft off-black ink — no gradient text, no twinkle spark — line one **"Record your product once,"** line two **"Let AI help your users."** (FINAL, founder sign-off) — both lines the same size, (founder amendment 2026-08-24, post-launch). Sub (founder's wording — they iterate it directly in the source between sessions; re-read the file before trusting this quote): FlowBuddy learns how your application works and turns that knowledge into the concrete outputs (in-app AI assistant, interactive walkthroughs, product videos, step-by-step guides), always in sync with your product. Value framing throughout is faster / cheaper / better on problems the buyer already has — never the category first.
- **The category story moved intact to `/application-intelligence-layer`** — the 2026-08-17 category hero verbatim (H1, sub, the layer-language captions on the shared flow diagram), the layer-in-your-stack explainer, a prose definition of "application intelligence layer" (the AEO land-grab now has a dedicated page to land on), and an everything-the-layer-powers grid including the direction-only consumers. Linked from the homepage (under the pinned card: "Under the hood, this is the Application Intelligence Layer"), the FAQ's category answer, and the footer.
- **The lifecycle is the argument.** The homepage's centrepiece walks one recording through three journey stages — **Activate** (New Users: interactive user onboarding, contextual help & guidance) → **Adopt** (Everyday Users: the in-app AI assistant, AI agent execution in beta) → **Train & Self-serve** (Power Users: autogenerated product videos, step-by-step SOPs, help portal).
- **Two agent claims, kept distinct on every surface.** FlowBuddy's own **in-product agent execution** is sold as **Beta**, always with the full trust framing: off by default, switched on per workspace, every run starts with the user's explicit consent, narrated, stoppable, outcome-verified. **Third-party agent access** stays *direction*, never available — the rule is the phase's own ([`interop.md`](../build/interop.md) states it; the roadmap says whether it still binds). It no longer appears on the homepage at all; the category page and `/future` carry it.
- **The Train stage's status framing (founder call 2026-08-24):** product videos, SOPs and the help portal are sold as **live on the page**. Videos are real (shipped, per-workspace flag). SOPs and the portal are ahead of the build — the founder accepted that tension for page copy, and the compensating rule is below.
- **The machine-readable guardrail, amended.** `SoftwareApplication.featureList` and `llms.txt` are read as bare claims of what exists, by engines that repeat them without the page's framing — and a signup can't find a help portal in Studio today. So the machine-readable surfaces deliberately do **not** list SOPs or the portal as shipped: `featureList` = the four live capabilities (agent marked beta) + product videos; `llms.txt` names videos live and keeps portal / SOP generation / third-party access as in development. Do not "sync" them to the page copy.
- **Scope of address:** "your SaaS" — the buyer is unchanged.

## 2. Page structure (as built)

**Homepage:**

1. **Hero** — the outcome claim (centered) + the shared full-width animated **flow diagram in three beats** (record your product → the knowledge is built from it, action by action → one trunk fans out to the four capability tiles, which are real links). Same diagram and loop as before the repositioning — only the captions and the beat-2 card label changed to buyer language ("Record your application once" · "FlowBuddy learns it — you approve it" · "It powers every user surface"; card: "Your Product Knowledge"). Design-system rules unchanged: product recreated in HTML on the tokens, never a fake photo; the markup ships the fully-lit end state so no-JS / reduced-motion / crawlers get the complete diagram; all continuous motion reduced-motion-gated.
2. **The user lifecycle** — the centrepiece scroll sequence, recast from "one intelligence layer." The pinned indigo card is now **"Your Product Knowledge"** (workflows · concepts · screens, "approved by you"), with the category link beneath it, and **seven consumers arrive along the rail grouped under three numbered stage headers** (① Activate · ② Adopt · ③ Train & Self-serve) whose badges sit on the journey line; on lg each header is sticky with a cascading offset — it pins while its own cards scroll under it and stays when the next stage pins beneath, so the finished section shows all three stacked (pure CSS; static below lg). All seven render as live cards (agent with the Beta pill); videos / SOPs / portal carry static branded previews (the video card is new; no greyscale treatment remains on this page). The section keeps its `#knowledge-base` anchor (nav "Use cases", footer, and the "See it in action" fallback aim at it). Scroll/pin mechanics and their tuning numbers are unchanged and owned by the source comments.
3. **How it works** — Record → Approve → Go live; copy speaks buyer language (no "structured application intelligence" on this page).
4. **Benefits** — the four outcome tiles, re-pointed at the lifecycle: higher activation · fewer support tickets · **lower content cost** · **always up to date**.
5. **Sign-up block** — copy is provisional; the final content is an open decision.
6. **FAQ** — leads with the category question (answer links the category page) and a new "What can one recording power?" lifecycle answer; keeps the beta acting answer and the two-claims agent answer linking `/future`.
7. **Footer** — the category page joins the Explore column; the blurb speaks the record-once claim.

**`/application-intelligence-layer` — the category page.** The 2026-08-17 category hero verbatim (H1 + sub + the shared diagram with layer captions) → the layer-in-your-stack explainer (Data → API → UI slabs + the lifted intelligence card) → the prose definition section ("What is an application intelligence layer?") → an eight-tile everything-the-layer-powers grid (four capability links · videos · SOPs · portal · third-party access as "In development") → the sign-up block. Breadcrumbs; no FAQPage JSON-LD (the category Q&A's structured data lives on the homepage — one question, one FAQPage entry).

**Top nav carries only the two section links** (use cases · how it works), in page order.

**Capability pages — four dedicated pages, one per live consumer** (2026-08-14): `/in-app-ai-assistant` · `/contextual-guidance` · `/interactive-onboarding` · `/ai-agent-execution`. Shared anatomy, capability-first then layer-story: a hero in the old homepage-hero layout (text left, looping HTML product demo right) → capability-specific how-it-works steps → a checklist detail band → a "powered by the application intelligence layer" band that mirrors the lifecycle section's pinned card and cross-links the sibling pages → the sign-up block → a page-scoped FAQ with its own `FAQPage` JSON-LD. Two decisions worth keeping: the **assistant page's hero is the retired assistant-first homepage hero, reused verbatim** — it is the assistant answering in context, which is that page's whole claim; and the **agent page sits inside the beta guardrail end to end** (Beta pill beside the hero eyebrow, consent/stop/verify copy, caption "In beta · Off by default · Every run needs your user's consent"). The homepage's diagram tiles and lifecycle cards link out, the footer's Product column lists all four, and the pages join `sitemap.xml` and `llms.txt`'s page list. The slugs live in one registry the tiles, footer and sibling links all read, so a renamed page moves everywhere at once.

**`/faq` — the full question set.** Same register rules as before, with rule 2 amended: acting is sold as **beta** with the full trust framing; what stays direction-only is **third-party** agent access. It opens with the FlowBuddy-AI-phrased identity answers ("What is FlowBuddy AI?" leads with what one recording powers; "What is an application intelligence layer?" is the definitional AEO entry, now backed by the category page).

**Deliberately out:** fake logos/testimonials · a pricing table ("free during early access" instead) · a standalone "what FlowBuddy is" prose section on the homepage — the definition lives on the category page now.

**The demo is the page itself:** the real widget embeds as the landing page's own overlay (FlowBuddy answering questions about FlowBuddy) — no demo section. The embed renders only when the build provides the landing widget key (names & defaults: `.env.example`), so the page never blocks on the dogfood KB existing.

**SEO/GEO on-page:** canonical + OG/Twitter meta (image alts included) with a branded `og.png` card rendered from the design tokens (headless-Chrome screenshot of an HTML template — re-render whenever hero copy changes materially; refreshed for the 2026-08-24 claim), JSON-LD (`Organization` + `SoftwareApplication` site-wide, `FAQPage` on every page carrying Q&As, `BreadcrumbList` on every subpage), `robots.txt` (explicit allow-groups for the named AI/LLM crawlers), `llms.txt` (the declarative product summary for generative engines — rewritten to the record-once/lifecycle framing), `sitemap.xml` with lastmod, a noindexed 404 page, declarative meta descriptions.

## 3. Open items

1. **Sign-up block (section 5) final copy** — placeholder-quality copy shipped; the founder decides the real content.
2. **Live-widget dogfood prerequisites:** record + approve a FlowBuddy-on-FlowBuddy KB in Studio, allowlist `flowbuddyai.com`, and set the widget key on the Render landing service.
3. **The brand name is half-migrated.** The visible wordmarks (header, footer), `/faq`, and the homepage `<title>` say **"FlowBuddy AI"**; the identity strings say **"FlowBuddy"** — `Organization.name`, `SoftwareApplication.name`, `og:site_name`, the `© 2026` line, and `llms.txt` throughout. Those are what search engines, link previews and generative engines treat as the entity's name, so the split is worth resolving deliberately rather than one surface at a time. The copyright line is a separate question again: it names a legal entity, not a wordmark.
4. **SOPs and the help portal are sold as live ahead of the build** (§1). The compensating machine-readable hold is in place; the real close-out is shipping them — until then, treat any new surface (ads, listings, outreach) the way §1 treats `llms.txt`, not the way it treats page copy.
5. **Off-page SEO/GEO** (content, articles, listings, Search Console/Bing submission) — untouched; a separate work stream.
